One optimisation job at a time, picked by its number (**#**, as on the Optimise page), with the answer first. Under the picker: the backend and method it searched with, and its objective.

**Save, rename or delete** the job picked with the buttons beside the picker. Every job is kept on disk as it runs; **Save** marks the ones worth keeping (★ in the lists), so **Delete the jobs not saved** on the Optimise page can clear the rest in one go. **Rename** changes its name in every list; its number stays. **Delete** removes the job with its trials, its backtest and its log, once you confirm; a running job has to be cancelled first.

**Best trial.** The best settings, each named as the strategy names it (*jma7 › length*), beside the value the job started from and the range it searched. Above them, the objective **in sample** (the history the optimiser searched, where the best trial is the best score) and **out of sample** (the history held back from it, run once with the best settings: the honest test), with trades and return for each. A big drop from in sample to out of sample means the settings fit the past rather than found an edge. Nothing is out of sample unless the job held some history back (set on the Optimise page).

When the held-back test made no trades, or fewer than the search required, the page says so, with what the backtest said and what to change. Its score then means nothing, and shows as a dash: a run that never trades has no Sharpe ratio. The test runs from the starting cash, as every search trial did, so a strategy sized as a share of a balance that grew in the searched years can be too small for the market's minimum stake in the held-back years, where the whole-history run still trades.

- **Apply to the strategy** writes the best values into the strategy and opens it on the Build page; the variables and their ranges stay.
- **Check it reproduces** re-runs the best trial to check the stored result still comes out the same, and works out the deflated Sharpe ratio: the chance the true Sharpe is above zero once the number of trials is allowed for.

Under the best trial, **Show** switches between two views: **the best trial's backtest** (the first) and **how the search went**. Only the one shown is drawn, so the page stays short.

**The best trial's backtest** opens on its own: the best settings' numbers by part, then a whole tear sheet for each (the job saved them as it finished; an older job runs them the first time and keeps them). In the table, *in sample* and *out of sample* are each run from the starting cash, as the optimiser scored them, so their numbers match the job's; *whole period* is one account from the first bar to the last; and *held back, inside the whole run* is that account's held-back part, starting from what it was worth there. **Tear sheet of** picks the part: every chart and metric the Backtest results page has, with a dashed line where the held-back history starts on the whole period's charts along the dates. **Log scale** draws the equity curve, the price chart or the Monte Carlo fan on a log axis, so equal percentage moves are the same height: a long history, or an account that grew a lot, reads better that way.

**Over random periods**, in the same view. The best settings run on many periods drawn at random from the history (or from only its searched or held-back part), each from the starting cash, as a box per number: annualised return, maximum drawdown, Sharpe ratio, trades, and the objective. A dashed line marks the whole history's value; its drawdown is over a longer time, so usually deeper. A narrow spread near it is a strategy that does not depend on when it started. Periods that made no trades are counted, with why, and left out of the boxes. They are kept with the job, so the same choices show at once next time.

**Walk-forward** jobs have no single best trial, so they show the search only, with each fold first: its in-sample and out-of-sample dates, scores and best settings, the out-of-sample to in-sample ratio, and the stitched out-of-sample equity (with a log scale too).

**How the search went.** How many settings were tried and how many were valid, how the neighbours of the best did (the stability check), how the search converged, and how the score varies with the settings (a pair heatmap, parallel coordinates, or one setting at a time).

**Every trial**, best first, with each setting named in words, the reason any trial is not valid, and what the backtest said: vectorbtX's notes on the trial's run, such as entries below the smallest size that were not placed, or entries cut to what the leverage funds. When no trial is valid, the page names the note most trials had, with what to change in the Studio. A trial that made no trades has no Sharpe ratio, and its cell is blank.

Saved backtests are compared on the **Backtest results** page.
