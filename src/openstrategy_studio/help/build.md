Work through the steps left to right. The **At a glance** panel on the right tells the whole strategy as it stands, as you would tell it: what is traded and how (*Spread bets on SPY, long only, on daily bars ...*), when it buys and sells, what each trade risks with its stop and take profit, what its features are and what is optimised. It then lists anything to fix before it can run. Each part of the strategy has its own colour: setup blue, features cyan, entry rules green, exit rules red, risk amber, the account purple.

The Studio starts with a blank strategy each time. **Start from** at the top gives a blank one again, or a ready-made template to edit.

**1. Setup.** Three cards:

- *Market*: name the strategy, pick the symbols, timeframe and dates.
- *Strategy*: what you are building, **trading on signals** (rules decide when to buy and sell) or **holding a portfolio** (target weights, rebalanced on a schedule or never; one symbol held from the start is buy and hold); and how you trade it: buying outright, CFDs, spread bets or FX spot. That sets the costs, how a short is charged and how positions are sized, for every symbol.
- *Instruments*: each symbol's terms, in three grids (market, costs, order sizes) showing only the fields that way of trading uses. **Every cell starts at its usual value** for the symbol's market, read from its ticker (EURUSD=X is an FX pair, ^FTSE an index in pounds, VOD.L a share in pence): the leverage (on spread bets and CFDs 30x for FX, 10x for indices and commodities, 5x for single stocks; none, 1x, bought outright, on FX spot and in every portfolio), the point size, a lot of 100,000 units on FX CFDs and FX spot, vectorbtX's spread and size steps, a usual slippage (in % of the price on each fill, on top of the spread: 0.01% for FX and indices, 0.02% for shares and commodities), and no other costs. A slippage above 1% a fill is flagged under the grid: a cost in points goes in the spread. Type your own to change any cell. Switching how you trade moves every value still at its usual setting to the new way's. A strategy with blank cells offers **Fill in the blank cells**. The borrow rate stays blank: no rate is published, so the run says when a short was borrowed for free.
- **Points.** Stops, targets, spreads and a spread bet's stake are counted in points, each instrument's point size of price. On a share a point is 1 cent, so a 1.00 move is 100 points (on a share priced in pence, 1p); on FX it is a pip (0.0001, or 0.01 on a yen pair); on an index, 1.0. A commodity's varies by broker, so check yours. The line above the grids says what a point is for each symbol, and the line under a stop or target box what its distance is in price.

**2. Features.** The lines and signals your rules compare. Each feature is a card: its name, type and formula on the title row. Click its **name** (the chevron beside it) to show or fold away its settings, and the **bin** to delete it. The dashed **Add a feature** card below them adds one in three steps: a category (Indicator, Statistical, Price levels, Candlestick, Chart pattern, Transform, Custom), a family (Moving averages, Momentum ...), then the item. Its outputs, the prices it reads and its settings (with their defaults) are listed before you add it, and it opens with every setting showing as soon as it is added. Each output becomes a line you can use, such as `bb.upperband`.

- *Price levels* holds the highest and lowest over n bars (with or without the current bar, and how many bars ago each was set), pivot points from the previous day, week or month, the previous period's open, high, low and close, and this day, week or month so far. *Price types* explain themselves: Typical price ((high + low + close) / 3) and so on.
- Rename a feature in its **Name** box at any time; the rules, other features and optimise variables that use it follow.
- Every input can read a price or another feature: an EMA of an RSI, or bands around an EMA.
- *Transform* holds the adjustments: offset a line by a percentage, a band either side of it, the distance between two lines, a line some bars ago, bars since a signal. "Crosses within 5% of the EMA" is a 5% band around the EMA, compared in a rule.
- On an SMA, EMA or other single-type average, **Let the optimiser pick the type** swaps it for the *Moving average* entry at the same type (the same numbers), so the Optimise page can try SMA against EMA and others.

**3. Rules** (signals). Four rules, each a card: long entry and short entry in green, long exit and short exit in red. A rule not used is one line with an **Add** button. A used rule's title row shows its logic line. Click the rule's **name** (the chevron beside it) to fold it away or open it again, like any card here. Beside it are a **versions** button (keep several versions of the rule for the optimiser to choose between) and a bin to delete it with everything in it. Inside, a rule is a list of named conditions and a **logic line** that combines them:

- **Add condition**, then open it and pick its **Kind**: compare two lines (*fast crosses above slow*), a pattern appears, the day, month or time of day, or a comparison on a higher timeframe. Every rule takes every kind, exits included: *condition1 or Friday* is a time exit.
- Each condition is a card inside its rule, titled in words, so a closed one still says what it does. Click its name to open or fold it; its bin deletes it, from the logic line too. A new one opens on its own.
- **Must hold** says when: on this bar, on each of the last N bars (*RSI above 50 for 3 bars*), on any of the last N bars (*a cross within the last 5 bars*), or N bars ago (*a cross 1 bar ago, then a close below the EMA now*). On a higher timeframe, N counts its bars.
- New conditions are named condition1, condition2 ...; type a name of your own in a condition's **Name** box (letters, digits and underscores, such as `trend` or `rsi_low`) and the logic line follows.
- The logic line uses `and`, `or`, `xor`, `nand`, `nor`, `xnor` and `not`, with brackets: `(trend and (cross or bounce)) and not overbought`. Brackets are needed wherever two different words meet, so the line always says exactly what you mean. Names match exactly, capitals included.
- What the optimiser searches in a condition (its comparison, a number, bars ago, the N of **Must hold** ...) is shown under it. Those values are added and their ranges set on the **Optimise** page, the one place for it. To search how many bars a condition must hold, pick *on each of* or *on any of the last N bars* first: N = 1 is the same as on this bar, so a search from 1 includes it.

**3. Portfolio** (portfolios). Optionally pick assets by a score, choose the weighting, and how often to go back to the target weights: never (buy and hold), every bar, or on a calendar, optionally only when the weights have drifted.

**4. Risk.** Amber sections, each folding away:

- **Position size**: two questions. *What sets the size?* A fixed size (shares bought outright, a stake per point on a spread bet, lots on FX, contracts on other CFDs), its value, or what it risks at the stop. *How much?* For a value or a risk: a fixed amount, or a share of the balance or of the equity (read at each signal bar, so trades grow with profits and shrink after losses). A share is a fraction: 0.01 is 1%, and the line under the box says what it is in money. A risk is sized from whichever stop you set, and with no stop from the leverage (the trade's margin is the risk, and no stop is placed).
- **Stop loss** and **Take profit**: each in points, in % of the price, as a multiple of a line (3 × an ATR) or at a line's level (an EMA, the signal bar's low); the line is read on the bar the signal fires on, and an entry whose level is on the wrong side of the fill is not placed. A trailing stop in points stays that far behind the best price; at a level, it moves to each completed bar's value and never loosens.
- **Leverage and funding**: each symbol's leverage and what it funds (set in the instrument grid on the Setup tab); whether a trade bigger than the account can fund is filled as far as it goes or skipped; and the **trade size check**: the largest trade your entries place, with its stop in price (implied by the leverage when there is no stop), against what the account funds, with what to change before you run. A share of the balance or equity is estimated here from the starting cash.
- **Extra exits** (after a number of bars, breakeven, a stepped stop; they need a fixed stop in points) and, for intraday bars, **Order timing**: entries good for the day only, and closing before the end of the day.

**5. Account.** Purple sections: the starting cash and reporting currency, how several symbols share the cash, interest rates for holding costs, and the engine's finer points (a random seed, what an opposite entry does).

**Preview** marks every signal on the chart; **Log scale** draws the price on a log axis, for a long history. **Pipeline** draws the strategy. **Strategy file** shows it as JSON and as a standalone vectorbtX script.

Optimisation ranges are not set here: add the values to search on the **Optimise** page, and set their ranges there.
