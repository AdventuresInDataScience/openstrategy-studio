**1. Choose the variables.** This page is the one place to choose what is searched; the Build page shows, under each condition, which of its values are. **Show** narrows the list to one part of the strategy. Add each value you want searched: a feature's setting (the EMA's period, or a moving average's type), a number in a rule (RSI below 30), a rule's comparison (crosses above or crosses below), a risk dial (the stop distance, the take profit, the bars a trade is held, the size and what a share of the account is of; whether there is a stop and which kind, whether there is a take profit, and how each is measured: in points, in % of the price, as a multiple of a line, or at a line's level), a portfolio setting, or which version of a rule to use.

A comparison swaps only with those that read the same sides: above, below, at or above, at or below and the two crosses with each other, and rising with falling (between swaps with nothing). Letting the optimiser pick the direction is choosing the idea after seeing the data, so judge it on held-back history or walk-forward.

For each, give **a range** (start, step, stop) or **a list of values**. The grid tries every step; the other searchers pick any value in the range, whole numbers for a whole-number setting. The count under each, and the total, show how big a full grid is.

Each part of this page is a section that folds by its title, so a long list of variables can be put away.

**2. Choose how to search.** One search, walk-forward, or a comparison of searches. Every backend is asked the same two things: the **backend** and its **method**. Optuna (TPE, CMA-ES, NSGA-II, random, quasi-random) is the usual choice; BUTChC and Optuna can also search between versions of a rule. Grid search runs one trial per backtest, or many per backtest for settings that change only the signals. SciPy offers differential evolution, dual annealing and DIRECT; Nevergrad offers particle swarm, CMA-ES, differential evolution, (1+1) and TBPSA, in batches on the worker processes. A backend with one method (random search, BUTChC, marsopt) shows it, fixed. A comparison races the backends and methods you pick, each several times. Every setting on this page is kept while the Studio stays open, so you can leave the page and come back to them.

**3. Set the objective and the guard rails.** The metric to maximise or minimise, a minimum number of trades (without one, optimisers find settings that trade twice and win both), and optional drawdown and profit-factor limits.

**4. Hold some history back.** The optimiser never sees the held-back part; the best result is then run on it once, from the starting cash, as each search trial is. Walk-forward does this repeatedly.

**5. Start.** **Start optimisation** first checks the ranges, unless they were just checked for these settings: it runs the strategy at its own settings, then each variable at the start and at the end of its range with the others at their own, on the history the search uses. An end that makes fewer trades than the minimum is named, since trials there count as invalid. When the strategy's own settings and every end all fall short, no trial is likely to be valid (a cost typed in the wrong unit, a size under the smallest size), so the job is not started: the page says what the backtests said, and **Start anyway** starts it regardless. **Check the ranges** runs the same check without starting. Each point's result is kept, so a check of the same strategy on the same data, after leaving the page or a restart, runs only the points it has not run before.

Jobs run in the background, with no window of their own, and keep going if you close the page. Under **Jobs**, each running job shows what it is doing, a bar of trials done, the best so far, how long it has run and about how long is left. The first trials take longest, while each worker process loads the backtest engine; more worker processes run more trials at once but each needs its own memory. A job whose process ended from outside (the computer restarted, or ran out of memory) is marked **stopped**, with what it had done, until you start another job; then its row says it stopped. Each finished job is a row: its number (**#**, the same on the Optimiser results page), its name, the backend and method it searched with, the objective, the best score, and its trials, with how it ended when that was not done (cancelled, failed, stopped). Most searches run a batch of trials side by side and wait for the slowest before choosing the next, so a batch smaller than the worker processes leaves some idle; dual annealing and mixed adaptive random search (marsopt) run one trial at a time. A tick under **Saved** marks a job saved on the Optimiser results page. **Delete the jobs not saved** deletes every other finished job, with its trials, backtest and log, once you confirm; running jobs are never deleted. Results are on the **Optimiser results** page, where **Apply to the strategy** writes the best values back, and where a job is saved, renamed or deleted on its own.
